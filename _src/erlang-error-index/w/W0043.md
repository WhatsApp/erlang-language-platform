# W0043 - Non-standard integer formatting

This diagnostic is experimental and disabled by default. Enable it via
`[linters.nonstandard_integer_formatting] enabled = true` and either
`experimental = false` or the `--experimental` flag, in the project's
[`.elp_lint.toml`](../../get-started/configure-project/elp-lint-toml.md).

## Warning

```erlang
fn() ->
    1000000.
%%  ^^^^^^^ 💡 information: Non-standard integer formatting
```

```erlang
fn() ->
    16#12AFBC31FF629986.
%%  ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^ 💡 information: Non-standard integer formatting
```

## Explanation

Long integer literals without separators for readability, or with separators
in non-standard locations can be error-prone to read.

To fix, add underscores to break the integer literal up into readable chunks.


```erlang
fn() ->
    1_000_000.
```

```erlang
fn() ->
    16#12AF_BC31_FF62_9986.
```

## Known limitations

The linter rewrites every base 2, 10 and 16 literal into a single canonical
form, without regard for what the number means:

- Decimal literals with four or more digits are grouped in thousands, including
  years (`2026` becomes `2_026`), ports (`8080` becomes `8_080`), dates
  (`20261102` becomes `20_261_102`) and times of day (`235959` becomes
  `235_959`).
- Existing underscores are discarded, so intentional groupings are lost:
  `2026_11_02` becomes `20_261_102`, and a binary literal grouped by bit field,
  such as `2#101_10110`, becomes `2#1011_0110`.
- Hexadecimal and binary literals are grouped in fours, so a colour such as
  `16#FFFFFF` becomes `16#FF_FFFF`.
- Lowercase hexadecimal digits are reported and uppercased: `16#ff` becomes
  `16#FF`.
- Leading zeros are removed: `0100` becomes `100`.
